How do continuous news channels decide what counts as news, and who gets to speak about it?

I work on the sociology of media and journalism with the methods of computational social science: large broadcast corpora, built and documented so that each measurement can be traced and reproduced.

The Laudisi Observatory

A pipeline I designed and maintain to record, transcribe and analyse French continuous news channels: stream capture, automatic speech recognition, speaker diarisation and OCR of on-screen banners.

Channels
BFMTV, CNews, franceinfo and LCI: on-screen banners on all four, speech on the first three
First corpus
March 2026, about 2,230 hours of broadcast
Methods
Topic segmentation, unsupervised clustering, sentence embeddings
Code
Project description on GitHub

The project examines agenda and framing dynamics across channels: how far they converge in the topics they cover and the words they use, which channel takes up a topic first, and who gets to speak on air, including the gap between how often women are seen and how often they are heard.
